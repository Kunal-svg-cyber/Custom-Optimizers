{\Large\bfseries Coordinate-wise SVRG for low signal-to-noise objectives}\hfill{\small Kunal}\\[1pt]
{\small One-page summary. Full details: \texttt{paper/technical\_report.pdf}; every claim and its limits: \texttt{docs/CLAIMS.md}.}

\textbf{What it is.} A variance-reduced optimizer with adaptive coordinate scaling, implemented in PyTorch and JAX, verified against an independent NumPy oracle, and studied on noisy finite-sum problems built to resemble tick data. The emphasis is on honest evidence: tuned baselines, held-out seeds, paired bootstrap intervals, and negative results reported as such.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Three implementations that agree.} PyTorch, JAX and a NumPy oracle follow the same update rule, checked step for step by automated tests (including adversarial-input fuzzing); on a Colab T4 the PyTorch engine stayed within $1.8\times10^{-7}$ of the oracle over 100 steps.
\item \textbf{Short proofs.} Unbiasedness and a variance bound for the control-variate gradient; a staleness bound valid for the clipped adaptive engine; a numerical-safety property; and a corollary of the Johnson--Zhang theorem that ties SVRG's rate to the \emph{preconditioned} condition number.
\item \textbf{A prediction from the theory, tested.} Jacobi-preconditioned SVRG with hyper-parameters read off the theorem and \emph{no tuning} reached float64 round-off on \ExpElevenBadWins{} seeds of a badly scaled problem, needing \ExpElevenSpeedup$\times$ fewer gradient evaluations than the project's own adaptive engine, with measured per-epoch contraction \ExpElevenContractionBad{} against the guaranteed \ExpElevenAlpha.
\item \textbf{Fourteen experiments} against learning-rate-tuned Adam and SGD with momentum (constant and cosine-decay), plus ablations of the engine's invariants.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What did not work, stated plainly.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Real data:} on three small real datasets with raw features no method reached the target and Jacobi SVRG led the best tuned baseline by only \ExpThirteenMinDiff--\ExpThirteenMaxDiff{} decades; after \emph{standardising} the features, SVRG variants reached the target on \ExpSixteenSvrgReached{} datasets and the baselines on \ExpSixteenBaselineReached{}, so the shortfall was raw-scale conditioning.
\item \textbf{Scope:} offline research, not a production trading component. A compiled single-sample SVRG step costs \LatSvrgOne{} at $d=32$ (inference dot product \LatDot), so the 2\,ms GPU step measures framework overhead, not the algorithm.
\item \textbf{No wall-clock win} over a direct solve at $n=\ExpFiveN$, $d=\ExpFiveD$ (normal equations \ExpFiveNormalSeconds\,s, SVRG \ExpFiveSvrgSeconds\,s); \textbf{no benefit} for walk-forward signal tracking (IC differences at most \ExpThreeMaxICDiff).
\item \textbf{Non-convex network:} sharper stationarity but worse held-out loss; with validation-tuned rates the deficit tracks adaptive scaling, not variance reduction. The adaptive engine has \textbf{no convergence proof}; \textbf{no market data} was used; \textbf{GPU wall-clock is unmeasured}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Corrections made along the way} (logged in \texttt{CHANGELOG.md}): a claimed benefit of clamping over additive $\varepsilon$ was retracted after an ablation; a ``best on all datasets'' statement was corrected to a tie; a missing convexity hypothesis was added to a theorem statement.
